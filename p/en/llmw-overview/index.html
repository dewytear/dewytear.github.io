LLM Wiki — the System and Capture

This material organizes the "LLM Wiki / Yohan Gu" talk paragraph by paragraph, in order. In the age of artificial intelligence, where and how you organize your own material matters enormously. We have already been extending context to AI with markdown files such as Claude Code's CLAUDE.md and memory.md, and Obsidian is a system that contextualizes all of it into a single framework.

Without heavy RAG or recompilation, AI follows the vault's map (signposts) to reach knowledge live.

What LLM Wiki is

It is a concept proposed by Karpathy in an April 2026 X post ("LLM Knowledge Bases") and the 'llm-wiki' gist: a system that lets artificial intelligence reference knowledge by embedding live, without heavy RAG that builds vector or graph databases and without continuous compiling. Once you build a knowledge base in an Obsidian vault, AI looks at the map and navigation (signposts) and finds its way to the knowledge.

The Vault System — Main Vault and Wiki Vault

Obsidian is fundamentally built on the vault system. A vault is a collection of markdown files bundled under a single name, and it is read as one set. It is the same structure as Claude Code opening a particular folder, reading the CLAUDE.md inside it, and taking on a distinct identity.

Separate knowledge written by AI from knowledge written by you

Wherever possible, it is best to separate knowledge written by AI from knowledge written by you into different vaults. It is easy to mistake what AI produced for something you fully understand yourself, so it matters to draw a strict line around how far your own study has actually gone.

Several wiki vaults (satellites) connect to the central main vault (mothership) through two-way sync and references.
VaultMetaphorWhat it holds
Main vaultmothershipConcepts you know well and can explain to others. Finished deliverables.
LLM wiki vaultsatellite (several of them)What you want to learn, what you do not know well yet, technical concepts. A storage space for raw material.

The speaker actually uses about 8 vaults, dividing them by purpose and by who they are shared with.

Local files and syncing

  • Obsidian works with local files, which is an advantage for work where security matters. File paths are exposed as-is, so computers handle them easily and quickly.
  • Avoid putting in files other than markdown (HTML, PDF, images, Hangul documents, and so on) — Obsidian's rendering slows down.
  • For syncing you can use the official Sync, or build a shared vault within an organization using a plugin such as osync, which mimics it and can be self-hosted.

What Goes In — Knowledge Capture

"If I just use a recent model with web search, why bother organizing things locally?" is a natural question. The answer lies in the limits of our own heads and in learning.

A forward base for learning — the stretched goal

The stretched goal from educational technology is the idea that learning happens smoothly only when you set a goal you can reach by stretching your arm a little further. The LLM wiki plays the role of this forward base for learning. You capture material you do not understand yet but want to learn, and inch forward with the help of AI.

Let AI know what you care about

Like the links and articles you pile up in KakaoTalk's "send to myself," we capture knowledge we have taken an interest in. Artificial intelligence does not know all of your tastes, but once you let it know where you showed interest and what you applied your human bias to, there is far more it can learn from there.

What goes into the main vault vs. the wiki vault

  • Main vault — concepts you know well enough to lecture on all day.
  • LLM wiki — concepts you want to talk about all day but do not know well yet. Often the more technical material.

For example, overseas cases researched while looking into mounting Starlink on a vehicle, or concepts learned while writing up BM25 (Best Matching 25, lexicon-based search), go into the wiki.

Key point What matters is organizing the concepts you intend to study in a form that not only you can read, but artificial intelligence can read alongside you.
