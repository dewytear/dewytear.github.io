AI 2040: Plan A — Reactions and Critiques

This installment follows the AI 2040: Plan A overview and gathers the support and criticism that appeared right after the 2026-07-09 release. What stands out is that the authors asked their sharpest critic (Richard Ngo) directly for a rebuttal and published it alongside the scenario — rather than suppressing the criticism, they ran it side by side. Richard Ngo's three objections (Selective Optimism)

Ngo has spent the past year as a consultant to the AI Futures Project critiquing and improving this scenario, and at the authors' request his remaining disagreements ran together with the launch. The three core points:

  1. The ambiguity of an “optimistic forecast” — AI 2040 is neither a pure prediction nor a pure recommendation but an “optimistic forecast,” so while it conveys a great deal of detail about a good future, readers find it hard to sort out how much of it the authors consider “desirable” and where that stops.
  2. The 2040 handover of power — in the core recommendation, humanity ultimately hands control of the world over to AI in 2040. Ngo sees this “handover” as faster than anyone else's.
  3. The politics of optimism — “how optimistic to be” is a political choice relative to the default outlook. The authors can justify this picture on the grounds that “slower progress is politically impossible,” but in doing so they end up advocating progress faster than almost anyone else.

The shared issue — if the premises collapse, so does the conclusion

Where the criticisms overlap is “the realism of the premises.” As Dave Friedman puts it, Plan A sketches a technically verifiable design, but whether that is politically feasible is another matter. Unless the premises hold — that the US and China trust each other, that they agree to place their nations' core compute in indefensible locations, and that the deal is sustained as a stable equilibrium — most of Plan A's conclusions do not follow. Rohit Krishnan likewise points out that “individual terms are unclear and underspecified” (what counts as AI R&D, what exactly “total transparency” is).
