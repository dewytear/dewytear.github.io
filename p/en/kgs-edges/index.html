How Documents Connect — Concept Curation and idf-Weighted Edges

The quality of a knowledge graph ultimately depends on what decides that "two documents are related". Instead of black boxes such as embeddings or statistical extraction, this wiki computes edges with verifiable rules, using the concepts a human read and left behind as the material. This document dissects those rules all the way through, with real numbers and a real edge.

The Material — Concepts Are Curated, Not Extracted

Every document has five or six key concepts curated for it when it is registered (step ③ of the pipeline in the previous part). One principle governs this: write only concepts that actually exist in the body. If you add a concept that is not in the body because you want a connection, the graph stops being "a map of real knowledge". So the edges of this graph are fewer in number, but each one can present its evidence (the shared concepts).

The Scale — The Rarer the Concept, the Stronger the Connection

When two documents share a concept, they become a connection candidate. But not every concept can weigh the same. Sharing 'skills', which appears in 11 of 64 documents, is close to coincidence, while sharing 'vector search', which appears in only 3, is a strong signal. So each concept is given a rarity weight (idf) — roughly the log of "total number of documents ÷ number of documents containing that concept":

The rarity scale — sharing a common concept counts light, sharing a rare one counts heavy.

Dissecting a Real Edge — Welcome ↔ "What Is Claude Code"

Take one edge from the actual graph apart: Getting Started and the Tutorial basics chapter share three concepts, 'terminal' (weight 3.48), 'CLAUDE.md' (1.98), and 'Claude Code' (1.87). The score is the sum of the weights, ≈ 7.33 — the same document's number-one related entry is the beginner tips with four shared concepts (≈ 9.56), so more shared and rarer concepts climb higher. The "Related docs" block at the bottom of a document shows these evidence concepts as chips, so you can always check why two documents were connected.

The Decision Rule — Three Branches

The decision flow — a coincidental single share is discarded, but a single share of a rare concept (in 3 or fewer docs) counts.
  • 2 or more shared → connect. Two different concepts overlapping is unlikely to be coincidence.
  • 1 shared + that concept appears in 3 or fewer documents → connect. A real case: the proposal overview ↔ the self-hosting public beta share only 'GitLab', but 'GitLab' appears in just 3 documents — rare enough to count. Conversely, a pair sharing only 'skills' is not connected — one concept shared by 11 documents cannot serve as evidence.
  • Otherwise → no connection by concept. Instead, a document that does not reach two concept connections is topped up with neighbors in the same System (e.g. the Basics chapter → the chapters before and after it in the same Tutorial), so that every document has at least 2 to 4 connections. The current ratio is 182 concept connections (77%) · 54 folder top-ups (23%).

The Ripple of a Threshold — Why You Cannot Add Concepts Casually

The "3 or fewer" rule has a subtle ripple. Suppose a rare concept currently appears in 3 documents and a new document uses that concept once more: the moment it becomes 4, an existing edge that hung on that single concept loses its qualification and is cut. That is why this wiki's rulebook (CLAUDE.md) forbids "adding a concept that is not in the body in order to remove an isolation" — one forced connection can cut a neighboring document's real connection. A document with thin connections is treated not as a defect but only as a review signal. This is this graph's honesty principle: edges are always grounded in the actual body, and curation is not a tool for making the graph look good.

Relations Declared by Curation — Directed, Typed Edges

The edges so far (related) are symmetric relations a machine computed automatically from shared concepts — if A and B are connected, it simply means "they are related", with no direction and no kind. But knowledge also has relations with direction and kind: "to read this, read that first", "this document implements that concept", "this article is the evidence for that claim". Such relations cannot be captured by shared concepts alone, so this wiki attaches typed relations declared by curation (relations) in a collection separate from related.

A directed typed edge — curation declares "to read this document, read that one first" (the reverse direction is derived automatically).

There are five terms in the vocabulary — prerequisite·implements·example-of·evidence-for·supersedes. Each relation carries a direction (A → target) together with a one-line justification (where it holds). It is stored only once in the forward direction; the reverse direction ("relations coming into this document") is derived automatically by scanning all documents. The "Knowledge relations" block at the bottom of a document shows outgoing and incoming relations with type badges.

A declaration looks like this — taking one record straight from the real data (an article serving as the evidence for a knowledge document):

{ "target": "sb-level4",              // the document this relation points to
  "type": "evidence-for",             // kind of relation: evidence
  "evidenceRef": "Notes on Jim Webber's Neo4j original (2024-06-04) —
                  expert evidence for sb-level4's knowledge-graph approach",
  "source": "human" }                 // curation origin (human-approved — not an auto suggestion)

The wiki currently declares 110 relations, all in this form. Most of them are reading-order prerequisite chains (previous document → next document) in the lecture-style Systems and the AI 2040 news series; the rest are implements, example-of, evidence-for, and cross prerequisites that cut across Systems. Real cases by type:

  • prerequisite — this very series is a chain: How Knowledge Accumulates → this document → Seven Views → How AI Reads It → Packaging as a Plugin. Each part is declared a prerequisite of the next, so the graph knows "what to read first". Getting Started → Extending Claude Code is the same type.
  • implements — Packaging the Wiki Engine as a Plugin implements the engine this series describes. Harness concept document → plugin architecture document belongs to the same line. There are bridges across Worlds too — the Douzone Knowledge Platform proposal declares that it will clone the "Reference Wiki (this wiki)" and implement this registration pipeline.
  • example-of — LLM Wiki is a concrete case of the stage described by Second Brain level 2 (grouping by topic). The first harness run also attaches as a concrete case of the harness-execution course.
  • evidence-for — as in the JSON above, an article that checked the external original attaches as external evidence for the claims of Second Brain level 4. Notes on Thariq's Fable field guide likewise backs the claim in harness engineering that "the environment, more than the model, decides the outcome" — four articles back knowledge documents with this type.

On top of this there is a third connection layer — citation backlinks (citedBy). The index build gathers in reverse the internal #! links a document body makes to other documents, and automatically shows them at the bottom of the cited document as “documents that cite this one”. Being neither shared concepts (related) nor declarations (relations), but a connection derived from traces of actually referring to something while writing, it fits the honesty rule naturally — and heavily cited documents reveal themselves as hubs on their own. Only documents in the knowledge index are counted as citers, so links from journals such as the Work Log are not mixed in.

The three layers differ in character. related is machine-made, symmetric, concept-based, so it spreads broadly; citedBy is machine-made, directed, body-link-based; and relations is curated, directed, typed, so it is sparse but dense in meaning. And they follow the same honesty principle — only relations that actually exist in the body are attached. In fact, since the current wiki has no case of "a new version superseding an old one that was kept" (documents are fixed in place), the supersedes type is left empty even though the vocabulary, validation, and UI are all in place — no forced pairs are made just to fill relations. Declared relations are guarded by the validators: a non-existent target (relations-dangling-ref) and a type outside the five-term vocabulary (relations-unknown-type) are ERRORs that block the merge, while missing justifications and conflicting declarations become WARN review signals.

In a nutshell Concepts are curated (body-real only) → edges come from rarity-weighted sharing (2 or more, or one rare concept) → shortfalls are made up by folder top-up → every document has 2–4 connections and there are no orphans. On top of that, citation backlinks (citedBy), the reverse index of body #! links, and directed typed relations (relations) declared by curation are layered separately. Heavily referenced documents become hubs and form the center of the graph (next part).
