Level 5 · The always-on brain OS

The last level takes everything so far (routing·wiki·vectors·graph) and adds "always-on". It is an autonomous brain OS that keeps absorbing and refreshing itself automatically without you having to think about it. The flagship example is GBrain.

What GBrain is A personal knowledge/memory layer built and released as open source by Garry Tan, president and CEO of Y Combinator. It is the operating system he actually runs his own agents (OpenClaw · Hermes) on, and it is said to hold a vast amount of information about pages, people, and companies.

What GBrain combines

PartWhat it is
StorageMarkdown-first + a Postgres backend
RelationsA self-wiring knowledge graph — every time a page is written it extracts entities and creates typed edges such as attended·works_at·invested_in·founded (without an LLM call)
SearchA hybrid of vector + BM25 (keyword) + RRF(rank fusion) + reranking
Always-oncron (scheduled jobs) auto-ingests meetings, email, tweets, and voice memos while you sleep

※ GStack, which comes up alongside it, is a toolkit that lets you use Claude Code like an "AI engineering team (CEO·designer·release manager·QA and so on)".

The always-on loop

The key point is that without a person feeding it every time, the system gathers material by itself, connects relations, reads first before the next answer, and writes back after answering.

A self-refreshing loop that runs without human intervention is the identity of Level 5.
Caution — the too-much-context dilemma "Always-on" has a trap. When there is too much context it gets in the way instead — at what point does the harm outgrow the help? That is why many people prefer to control what gets absorbed themselves ("just pull in this week's meeting notes, let's brainstorm together and then put them in together"). Be careful that automation does not turn into noise automation.
Once more — Level 5 is not the best Constant syncing is only worth it when you truly need it (many offline agents, bulk data, team-scale automated pipelines, and so on). The burden of managing cron yourself is heavy too. What you ingest and what you leave matters even more, and the criteria for that call are laid out in Context vs Connections.
