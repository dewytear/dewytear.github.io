Understanding the Harness Through a Kitchen Metaphor

Harness engineering is easy to understand when likened to running a kitchen.

Solo chef vs. multi-agent

A solo chef is fast but a bottleneck appears, while a multi-agent kitchen splits the roles and works long while keeping the quality.
  • Solo chef — ordering, cooking, checking and dishwashing all alone. Fast at first, but once orders pile up a bottleneck appears and it is hard to work for long.
  • Multi-agent — separate the roles into ordering, prep, cooking and checking. In parallel at the same time, each focusing on what it does well → it works long while keeping the quality.
ConfigurationUpsideDownside
Single agentFast and economicalLimits in scaling · stability
Multi-agentStable, highly scalableCosts more

It is not a cure-all. There are tasks for which a one-person kitchen is enough, and separately there are tasks where multi shines.

Harness = running the whole kitchen

Who does what and when is designed in advance, and agents · skills · orchestration are tightly connected. Managing that workflow and those rules well overall is harness engineering.

Quality has to be refined continuously

You have to review and give feedback both midway and at the end — whether the ingredients are fresh (the intermediate outputs) and whether the final dish is fine. Even with a harness, a finished product does not come out in one shot. Treat what comes out as a draft, and keep refining the agents to raise it to high quality.

Token usage Because you run it multi, token usage goes up. It grows especially when the agents talk to each other, and in measurements there were cases of using about 30% more.
