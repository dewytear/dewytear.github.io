Level 4 · Linking by relationship (knowledge graph)

Where Level 3 found "things close in meaning," Level 4 follows "what is tied to what, and how." That is the knowledge graph. The question takes the shape "start at X and trace the connections through to A." It is usually the most powerful but also the most complex and costly stage.

Entities + relationships = a graph

A knowledge graph is made of two things: entities (people, companies, products) and the named relationships between them (who works where, what endorses what). Unlike wiki links, the relationship carries meaning (a type).

You assemble the answer by following a chain of relationships, as in "Jordan → Acme → Postpilot → Cadently."
It can be lighter than a wiki A wiki makes you read related pages in full (as in "reading the entire AI video production document when all you need is 11Labs"). A graph only needs you to touch the edges (relationships) you need, so in such cases it is actually lighter.

Tools — LightRAG · Graphiti · GraphRAG

Usually software extracts the entities and relationships automatically and builds the graph for you. The notable ones:

ToolCharacteristics
LightRAGLightweight RAG combining graph and vectors. Strong at incremental updates (open source).
GraphitiA knowledge graph framework that also captures time (change).
GraphRAG (Microsoft)An approach that builds entities, relationships, and summaries from a body of documents.

※ The lecture's "graphity-fire" appears to mean Graphiti, and "quadrant" to mean the Qdrant of Level 3.

The real issue is not search but "getting what is in your head out"

The real bottleneck is usually not that the system cannot find things, but that the knowledge in your head has not gone into the system yet.

Use an "interview me (grill me)" style skill to dig into your customers and business and turn it into a file, and that becomes material for the graph and the wiki.

So before blaming the system, ask yourself first: "Are my folders really as holistic as what is in my head?" The habit of feeding in materials, contracts, and subtitles while fleshing them out through brainstorming is the key.

Privacy — where is the data going Processing your data with a Claude model means the content is sent to Anthropic. That may be acceptable for your own work materials, but it is a different story for customer data or sensitive information. In those cases consider running open-source or local models and keeping the Second Brain that holds everything local rather than in the cloud. Being aware yourself of what gets processed where comes first.
