The five levels at a glance

Second Brains divide into five levels by "how well you find and recall". Each level answers a different question. The ladder below means smarter search as you go up, but it also means more complexity and higher cost.

Higher up is more powerful, but "powerful" does not mean "needed".

The question each level answers

Find exactly

Level 1Files · routing

"That word or file I wrote — where is it?" — you point straight at it with a CLAUDE.md acting as a router plus a folder structure.

When — when you re-explain often and search by exact name

Group by topic

Level 2Wiki · memory

"Gather everything about this topic." — you walk down the LLM wiki's index and weave the related notes together.

When — 30+ notes and you keep forgetting what you had

Find by meaning

Level 3Vector · embedding

"Find something close in meaning even if the words differ." — semantic search (a vector DB) looks at similarity by meaning.

When — when notes that exist get missed over word mismatches

Connect by relationship

Level 4Knowledge graph

"Start at X and follow the connections to A." — you trace chains of relationships through a knowledge graph of entities and relations.

When — when "who is tangled with what, and how" matters

Autonomous brain OS

Level 5always-on

"Stay updated on your own without me minding it." — an always-on brain that keeps absorbing meetings, mail, and memos automatically (cron).

When — when offline agents or bulk data need constant syncing

The most important principle Choose the lowest level that matches your pain. If nothing is uncomfortable right now, there is no reason to climb. And the level may differ from folder to folder even within a single project — meeting minutes at level 3, the project folder at level 1, for instance.
