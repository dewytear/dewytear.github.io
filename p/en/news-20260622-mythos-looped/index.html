Anthropic’s Mythos and the Looped Transformer — reading the Heisenberg column

Starting from “Why Anthropic’s Mythos Is Good News for Korean Companies,” a column contributed to the science and technology outlet Heisenberg, this document sorts out what the core technology the article points to — the Looped Transformer — actually is, and how far the claim that it is “good news for Korean companies” is confirmed fact and where it becomes conjecture. Because the purpose of this piece is not to mix fact with conjecture, what is verified and what is commentary or conjecture are marked section by section.

Article info (sources) · Title: Why Anthropic’s Mythos Is Good News for Korean Companies
· Outlet: Heisenberg (heisenberg.kr) · Author: DION · Published: 2026-06-22
· Original: heisenberg.kr/claude-mythos — the body is behind a paid membership; the quotation below is excerpted from the publicly available “one-minute summary.”

The article’s argument

The article holds that inside “Mythos” — Anthropic’s top-end model, parts of which were sealed off because it was too powerful — there is a Looped Transformer, and that because of the efficiency of that structure, top-grade AI that runs directly on a device without the cloud becomes possible, which is an opportunity for Korean companies strong in device manufacturing. The key passage of the public summary reads as follows (quoted verbatim).

“The technology said to be hidden inside it is the ‘Looped Transformer.’ Where existing AI solves problems by writing its intermediate thoughts down as text, this structure writes nothing down and chews over the same circuit dozens of times purely in its head. That makes it faster and cuts token costs too. In particular, one recent paper proved mathematically for the first time that with this approach a model with half the parameters nearly reaches the level of an ordinary model. Half the size for the same performance means top-level AI can run directly, without the cloud.”

— Heisenberg, “Why Anthropic’s Mythos Is Good News for Korean Companies,” one-minute summary (2026-06-22)

What a Looped Transformer is — a structure that repeats one block

An ordinary transformer creates depth by stacking N layers, each with different weights. A Looped Transformer does the opposite: it repeats a single weight-sharing block N times, feeding that block’s output back in as its input. Instead of stacking new layers it passes through the same circuit many times, so computational depth is decoupled from parameter count — a small model can imitate a far deeper stack.

The standard stacks different layers once each; the looped version passes through the same single block many times.

The operation laid out by recent research (the Huginn line in the sources below) has three stages: Prelude → Recurrent Block → Coda. The prelude embeds the input into latent space (e), the recurrent block updates the latent state s several times while mixing the input e back in at every iteration, and the coda turns the final state back into output. The number of iterations is not fixed but can vary with the difficulty of the input (adaptive depth), so the harder the problem, the longer it “chews things over” internally.

Looping in latent space (s) — chewing thoughts over internally without writing them down as text (tokens). The harder it is, the more it loops.

This is the point the article means by “it does not write its intermediate thoughts down as text.” Unlike today’s reasoning models, which spell their thinking out at length as text (a chain of thought), the looped approach refines a continuous latent state by repetition. As a result, reported advantages are that (1) it emits fewer output tokens, cutting token cost, (2) it does not need separate chain-of-thought training data, and (3) deep reasoning is possible even with a small context window. The article’s phrase “similar performance with half the parameters” also connects to the results of this line of research: weights are shared to save parameters, while the effective depth is made up for by repetition.

Why is it ‘good news for Korean companies’ — the article’s argument (commentary and conjecture)

The article’s chain of inference goes like this: similar performance with half the parameters → top-grade AI can run directly on the device (on-device) without a heavy cloud → this favors Korea (Samsung, LG, Hyundai), which is strong at making smartphones, cars, and robots. The view is that Korea can play to its strengths on the device side rather than in the competition over cloud infrastructure.

This part is ‘commentary’ “Good news for Korean companies” is not Anthropic’s official position but the article’s interpretation. In fact, the point that the top-performing model (Fable 5) has become generally available on the major clouds — AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft and others — so that any company can use it, together with the close Korea–Anthropic ties in the “confirmed facts” below, can be seen as grounds for this argument. On the other hand, no official material supporting on-device deployment of a Mythos-class model has been confirmed.

Confirmed facts

  • Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 released (2026-06-09) — the two are the same model, except that Mythos relaxes some dual-use (cyber, bio) safeguards and is provided only to a small number of vetted organizations (Project Glasswing). Fable 5 is generally available through the API and the major clouds.
  • Export controls and their lifting — on 2026-06-12 the United States applied export controls to Fable 5 and Mythos 5, halting them for about 19 days; the controls were lifted and redistribution resumed on 2026-06-30–07-01.
  • Samsung Electronics and SK hynix invest in Anthropic’s Series H (reported late May 2026) — participating as strategic infrastructure partners.
  • Samsung Foundry in talks to manufacture Anthropic’s custom AI chip (reported 2026-07-03) — covering a 2nm process and advanced packaging.

→ The firmest grounds for the “good news for Korean companies” argument are these actual partnership and investment flows, rather than the on-device hypothesis.

How far is it fact — conjecture and unconfirmed

“Mythos = Looped Transformer” is not officially confirmed Anthropic has never disclosed that Mythos uses a recurrent-depth (looped) structure. This connection is a hypothesis that came out of things like OpenMythos (Kye Gomez), a community project that conjecturally reconstructs the Mythos architecture from public literature, and those reports themselves state that it is “unconfirmed.” The article’s premise (that there is a looped structure inside Mythos) should therefore be read as interesting conjecture, while the Looped Transformer technology itself is verified as real by the papers below.

This document’s stance The technology called the Looped Transformer is confirmed to be real by papers, and the partnership and investment between Anthropic and Korean companies are facts too. But it is more accurate to read “Mythos uses that technology” and “therefore it is good news for Korea” separately, as conjecture and commentary rather than official confirmation.
