Level 2 · Grouping by topic (LLM wiki + memory)

Once files pile up, you start wanting to weave them together by topic. That is where the LLM wiki comes in. The question at this level is "gather everything about this topic." Think of it as laying an index and links on top of Level 1.

Ties to a topic already covered This wiki already has an LLM Wiki set that covers the Karpathy-style LLM wiki in detail. Level 2 is exactly that world — here we only touch on the key points from a Second Brain perspective.

A wiki is "reading your way down the index"

A wiki's strength is its index. When AI enters the wiki, it goes "they asked about agentic workflows, so start here → drill down into the related WAT framework → and if needed go all the way to the CLAUDE.md system prompt," reading whole pages as it follows the trail.

Tell the AI to "put this in the wiki" with YouTube subtitles or meeting notes and it sorts them automatically into concepts, comparisons, sources, and techniques to build a structure like this.

Memory — a file the AI writes on its own

The new axis Level 2 adds is memory. Turn on Claude Code's auto memory and the AI keeps memory.md updated by itself. Context accumulates without you paying attention.

# Check whether auto memory is on or off
/memory
# If you see an auto-memory on / off state, just turn it on when it is off

Tool independence — Claude Code or Codex

A Second Brain is just files and folders, so it is not tied to one tool. The file-naming conventions do differ per tool, though.

ToolInstruction fileMemory
Claude CodeCLAUDE.mdmemory.md updated automatically
Codex, etc.AGENTS.md (copy the same content into it)Route it with "for memory, look at memory.md"

※ Copy CLAUDE.md over as AGENTS.md and both tools share the same brain. In the end it comes down to where you tell them to look (routing).

Wiki links = knowledge graph? No Links in a wiki are closer to "see also" and backlinks. They do not carry the meaning of a relationship, as in "this endorses that / this is a competitor of that." The effect is similar, but truly relationship-based is Level 4 — the knowledge graph.
Obsidian is not a requirement Obsidian, famous for its flashy graph view, is in the end just a layer that visualizes markdown. Use it if seeing things visually helps, and skip it if not — what matters is whether your system finds and pulls things out for you. Next comes Level 3 — searching by meaning.
