AI 2040: Plan A — the year-by-year scenario (2027 → 2040)

This installment follows on from the AI 2040: Plan A overview and lays out the world the scenario paints year by year. Keep in mind that Plan A is not a prediction but a recommendation — it is a story about “how things ought to go,” not “how things will go.” What follows is the flow as cross-checked against published summaries and reporting, and the detailed figures follow the model in the original.

Before you read The scenario starts from the premise that “without intervention now, superintelligence arrives in 2030.” All of Plan A’s machinery is focused on pushing that moment back ten years to buy time to put safety infrastructure in place. The details of the supporting machinery are in the policy and verification installment.

2027 — two workforces

At the scenario’s starting point the United States has two workforces. One is 165 million people; the other is AI agents — a population of which millions are created and terminated every hour, working ceaselessly at superhuman speed. Around this time (2027–2029) governments begin applying gating to frontier models.

2028 — the election and data centers

The 2028 election is heated as ever, and AI becomes the biggest issue. At this point the cost of the data centers under construction reaches twice the entire US defense budget. The same disruption of office work as a whole that software engineering went through in 2026 spreads, and many jobs turn into “managing AI agents.” AI companies industrialize the training process, automating occupations one after another: once executives decide “this year let us go into [some particular profession],” they interview experts, buy up data and build a training environment.

2029 — the US–China agreement

The United States and China agree to avoid a reckless superintelligence race. The two countries choose a Total Research Transparency approach as the high-level regulatory and transparency regime to aim for. This agreement is the branch point of the whole scenario.

2030 — superintelligence averted

On the original trajectory, this is the year AI R&D would have been fully automated, reaching superintelligence by year’s end. In Plan A this leap is deliberately averted thanks to the 2029 agreement.

2030–2035 — expansion within the human range

In this stretch AI grows gradually stronger within the range of human ability, reaching roughly top-human-expert systems. The key is to build capability without crossing into the superhuman domain.

2035 — the pause

Development is paused at the expert level. The purpose is a single one — keeping humans in control. Time is allowed for safety and verification infrastructure to mature sufficiently before moving on to stronger systems.

2040 — the release, and superintelligence

On the premise that safety infrastructure is in place, the pause is lifted and capability is at last scaled up to superintelligence. This is why the name is “AI 2040” — because it is the scenario that pushed to 2040 a superintelligence that could have come in 2030.

Fact and estimate The year-by-year substance (the two workforces, the 2029 agreement, the 2030 aversion, the 2035 pause, the 2040 release) is the scenario’s account as confirmed by published summaries and reporting. But this is only the trajectory the authors wish for, not a prediction, and premises such as “superintelligence arriving in 2030” are themselves the core point of contention in the critiques.
