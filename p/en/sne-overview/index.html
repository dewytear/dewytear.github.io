Send Engine Implementation Request — Overview and Responsibility Boundary

AI Notification is complete up to deciding "what · when · to whom · through which channel" to send and producing the send request. We ask the Platform team to implement a Kafka (message queue) based send engine that will take over everything after that — the actual sending, timing, reprocessing, and status.

Purpose of this document This document requests that the sending stage be implemented as a shared send engine. It is organized conceptually around what the Platform team needs to build and what AI Notification needs; it does not contain a concrete internal design (details to be settled through discussion).

Overview

The AI Notification system takes business events and user requests, applies rules to decide which notification · at what moment · to whom · through which channel to send, and keeps the result in the form of a "send request". That is the AI Notification scope completed in the first implementation phase.

What is needed now is a send engine that takes those send requests and actually sends them, executes the timing at the right moment, reprocesses failures, and manages and displays the send status. Since high volume, asynchronous processing, delivery guarantees, and scalability are required, we ask that it be implemented on Kafka.

Request overview — required characteristics of sending, and the responsibility boundary

The sending stage calls for the following characteristics. (The choice of queue technology and the topology are left to the Platform team's judgement; the request assumes a Kafka base.)

  • Asynchronous · surge absorption — separate the decision from the sending, and buffer momentary bursts of sends in the queue.
  • Delivery guarantee — send without loss, and confirm whether the message actually arrived.
  • Reprocessing — retry transient failures automatically; isolate repeated failures and reprocess them manually.
  • Accurate timing — send according to the timing type: immediate, delayed, recurring, transition.
  • Scalability — keep up as channels and volume grow.
